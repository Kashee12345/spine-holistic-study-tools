Part 1: Autonomic Balance Simulator

Teaching modelA simplified model of one healthy adult across one day. A stressor, such as an exam or a work deadline, happens from 9 to 11 am each day. The settings shape autonomic balance, heart rate, HRV and cortisol. Real people vary a lot; this is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.

Simulator readouts at the selected time
MeasureValue

How the model works: sympathetic and parasympathetic activity are each scored from 0 to 100 percent. They depend on the time of day, sleep, the daily stressor, how long the stress has lasted (which builds allostatic load), sleep debt and recovery activities. Heart rate and HRV (RMSSD) follow the balance; the ECG beats are generated from that heart rate with breathing linked variation. Cortisol follows a daily rhythm with a peak after waking at 7 am, a rise after the stressor, and a flatter shape with long lasting stress and short sleep. Simplified for learning.

Recovery activities: exercise, time outdoors, time with friends, mindfulness or other things that help you unwind.
