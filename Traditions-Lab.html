Part 1: Testing a Tradition

Teaching modelA simulated randomized trial. Each dot is one fictional participant with chronic pain scored 0 to 10. You set how much people recover naturally, how much expectation and the treatment ritual help, and how big the true specific effect is. The trial then shows what each comparison can and cannot tell you.

Trial readouts at the selected week
MeasureValue

How the model works: improvement in each arm is natural recovery (all arms) plus the nonspecific effect of expectation and the treatment ritual (real and sham arms) plus the true specific effect (real arm only), each building over 12 weeks, plus random person to person variation (standard deviation 1.5 points). For the nature walk, the comparison is a city walk; people know where they walk, so only about half of the expectation effect is matched. Uncertainty ranges are 95% confidence intervals.     Part 2: Herb Drug Interaction Bench

    Teaching modelDrug levels are in relative units: 100 is the usual average level and 70 to 140 is the working range. No real doses are shown. This is not advice about any medicine or product.

    Bench readouts at the selected day
    MeasureValue

    How the model works: a dose every 12 hours keeps the drug in a repeating pattern. The average level is proportional to the dosing rate divided by clearance. St. John's wort induces CYP3A4: clearance rises over about a week (3 day time constant) and falls back over about a week or two after stopping (4 day time constant). Grapefruit juice, a food shown for contrast, blocks CYP3A4 in the gut and works within a day. Ginkgo is modelled only as a possible added bleeding tendency; the evidence for that is mixed. Simplified for learning.
