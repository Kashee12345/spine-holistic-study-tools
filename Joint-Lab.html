Part 1: Joint Gapping Simulator

Teaching modelA simplified model of one knuckle being pulled apart by a cable, as in laboratory studies. It shows how pull force and pull speed change joint separation and the pressure inside the joint, when a gas cavity forms, and why the joint cannot pop again for about 20 minutes. Numbers are rounded teaching values. This is not a guide to cracking joints or to any treatment.

Simulator readouts
MeasureValue

How the model works: before a cavity forms, separation rises with force at about 1 mm per 100 N. Separating the sealed joint lowers the fluid pressure, while the capsule and fluid slowly adjust (a time constant of about 1.5 seconds), so a quick pull drops the pressure further than a slow one. When the pressure falls about 6 kPa below its resting level, a gas cavity forms, the pull force dips for an instant and the surfaces jump apart; after that the joint separates easily. The gas dissolves back over about 20 minutes, and until then no new cavity can form. Simplified for learning; real joints and people vary.
